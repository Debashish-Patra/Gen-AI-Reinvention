# FAQ and glossary

Quick answers to common questions, then a plain-language glossary of every term used on this site.

---

## Questions

### The basics

**What is this framework?**
A simple way to plan and run Gen AI change across a business. It has five pillars (what to cover) and four phases (when to do it), with a decision gate at the end of each phase. See the [home page](index.md) and [the idea](idea.md).

**What is the difference between a pillar and a phase?**
Pillars are workstreams that run side by side. Phases are the calendar. Every phase has a job for every pillar, which gives the twenty cells in the [pillars and phases grid](axes.md).

**Why five pillars?**
Because Gen AI programmes fail for different reasons, and each pillar guards against one: no clear value, people left out, unmanaged risk, weak foundations, or no plan for change after launch. See [the five pillars](pillars.md).

**Is it only for large companies?**
No. A small team can run the same four phases with a lighter touch: one page for the ambition, a short scoring session, a two-week pilot. The order and the gates matter more than the size.

**Is it specific to marketing, retail or travel?**
No. Marketing is the running example because it has clear numbers and many repeatable tasks. The [marketing example](example-marketing.md) uses a fashion retailer and a regional airline, and the same steps work for finance, service, operations or HR.

**Are the examples real?**
No. Every company, number and result on this site is fictional and exists only to show how the method works. Replace them with your own data.

### Starting out

**Where do I start?**
With Frame. Write the ambition in one sentence, record baselines, and name a sponsor. Use [template 1](toolkit.md#1-ambition-and-baselines).

**How long does it take to get to a pilot?**
About eight weeks for Frame and Prioritize, with the pilot running over the following months. Timings are indicative. A small scope can move faster.

**Who should be in the room?**
An executive sponsor with budget authority, a Finance partner, the function leads whose work will change, and someone who owns risk. Add the people who do the work as early as you can.

**What if we have no data or tools ready?**
Then readiness scores will be low, and that is useful to know. Pick a use case that needs little (content drafting, for example), and build the foundation for the harder ones in parallel. See [Pillar 4](pillars.md#pillar-4-digital-core).

### Choosing and scoring

**How do we pick the first use case?**
Choose one with high value and high readiness that is small enough to finish. Use the [scoring grid](toolkit.md#2-use-case-scoring-grid).

**What if the biggest prize scores low on readiness?**
Do not drop it. Mark it as a long-term investment and build what it needs while a smaller pilot proves the method. The marketing example does exactly this with next-best-offer and next-best-action.

**What is the difference between a table stake and a strategic bet?**
A table stake is something competitors already do, so you need to do it well but not argue about it. A strategic bet is where you hope to gain an edge and accept more uncertainty.

**Should we pilot several things at once?**
Only if they share one build, as email copy and product descriptions do in the example. Several unrelated pilots stretch the team and blur the lessons.

### Business cases

**Why have an optimistic and a pessimistic case?**
Because a single number hides the risk. If the pessimistic case still clears the hurdle, the decision is easy. If it does not, you know exactly what has to go right.

**What hurdle should we use?**
Whatever your Finance team already uses for investments, agreed before the numbers are calculated. The example uses a positive NPV in the pessimistic case and breakeven within 30 months. Yours may differ.

**Where do the assumptions come from?**
From your baselines, your own cost data, and early tests. Be honest about which are measured and which are guesses. The pilot should replace guesses with measurements before Gate 3.

**Can I use the spreadsheet model for my own project?**
Yes. [Template 3](toolkit.md#3-business-case-worksheet) is in the `templates` folder, pre-filled with the fictional retail example so you can see it work before you overwrite the inputs.

### Piloting

**Why do we need a control group?**
Without one, you cannot tell whether a result came from the AI or from something else, such as a seasonal change. A control group turns "it feels faster" into evidence.

**How big should the pilot be?**
Big enough that the difference between the pilot and the control group is not just noise, and small enough to finish in about twelve weeks. One product category, market or team, and one channel, is a good start.

**What should a person always approve?**
Anything customers see, anything that makes a claim about price, policy, health or performance, and anything using personal data. In the example, a person approves every output twice, at review and at publish.

**What if the pilot fails?**
That is a valid result. Gate 3 can say stop, and a weak idea stopped early saves the cost of scaling it. Write down what you learned and move to the next idea.

### Risk and people

**How do we manage risk without slowing everything down?**
Screen early, apply light controls to low-risk work, and keep heavy controls for the cases that need them. See [Pillar 3](pillars.md#pillar-3-responsible-ai) and the [risk register](toolkit.md#4-risk-screen).

**Will this replace jobs?**
Roles change in different ways: some are augmented, some shift toward new tasks, and a few are new. Use the [role impact map](toolkit.md#5-role-impact-map) to plan it openly, and say what freed time is for. Honest planning builds trust.

**How do we keep people engaged?**
Involve them in choosing the use cases, train them on the real workflow, share prompts in a community of practice, and show results. See [Pillar 2](pillars.md#pillar-2-talent-and-new-ways-of-working).

### After the pilot

**What does "scale" actually mean?**
Extending the scope in waves (more categories, markets and channels), reusing what was built, and moving the workforce through the change. See [Phase 4](phases.md#phase-4-scale-and-reinvent).

**What happens after the programme is scaled?**
A quarterly review keeps going: value against the business case, adoption, incidents, and which idea enters the pipeline next. This is the loop in [Pillar 5](pillars.md#pillar-5-change-and-continuous-reinvention).

**How do I know when to stop an idea?**
When it fails a gate and a fix will not change the answer, or when the quarterly review shows value falling short of the case. Retire it, keep the lessons, and free the budget.

## Glossary

| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| **Ambition** | A one-sentence statement of what the programme is for, for example "more personalised campaigns at lower content cost". |
| **Baseline** | The number for a measure today, recorded before anything changes, so improvement can be proved later. |
| **Breakeven** | The first month in which total benefit so far is greater than total cost so far. |
| **Business case** | A short model of costs and benefits for one idea, with an optimistic and a pessimistic case. |
| **Centre of excellence** | A small team that holds standards, tools and know-how, and helps other teams use them. |
| **Community of practice** | An informal group that shares prompts, tips and lessons across teams. |
| **Control group** | A comparable group doing the work the normal way during a pilot, used as the point of comparison. |
| **Digital core** | The data, platforms, tools and security that Gen AI use cases depend on. |
| **Discount rate** | The yearly rate used to turn future money into today's money when calculating NPV. |
| **Foundation** | The minimum data, tools and controls a pilot needs. Built only as far as the pilot requires. |
| **Frame** | Phase 1. Align on ambition, baselines and sponsor. |
| **Gate** | A decision point at the end of a phase, with three outcomes: go, fix or stop. |
| **Gen AI** | Generative AI. Software that creates text, images, code or other content from instructions. |
| **Guardrail** | A control that limits what an AI system can do or say, such as an approved-claims check. |
| **Hurdle** | The minimum result a business case must reach to go ahead, agreed in advance. |
| **Human in the loop** | A person reviews or approves the AI's work before it has an effect. |
| **Long-term investment** | A high-value, low-readiness idea that waits for its foundation. |
| **Net present value (NPV)** | The total of all future net benefits, each reduced for the time it takes to arrive. Above zero means the idea creates value after the cost of money. |
| **Pessimistic and optimistic cases** | Low and high sets of assumptions used to show the range of likely results. |
| **Pillar** | One of five areas that must all be covered: Lead with Value, Talent and New Ways of Working, Responsible AI, Digital Core, and Change and Continuous Reinvention. |
| **Phase** | One of four steps in time: Frame, Prioritize, Build and Pilot, and Scale and Reinvent. |
| **Pilot** | A small, time-limited test with a control group and human review. |
| **Prioritize** | Phase 2. Score ideas, build business cases and choose the pilot. |
| **Prompt** | The instruction given to a Gen AI system. |
| **Readiness** | How prepared the organisation is to deliver an idea: data, tools, skills and policy. |
| **Reviewer rejection rate** | The share of AI outputs a human reviewer sends back. A falling rate suggests the system and the team are improving. |
| **Responsible AI** | Using AI in ways that are safe, fair, transparent and accountable, with risks screened and controlled. |
| **Return on investment (ROI)** | Net benefit divided by cost. |
| **Risk register** | A list of risks for each use case, with the control, owner and level before and after. |
| **Scale and Reinvent** | Phase 4. Extend in waves, reskill people, and keep improving with a quarterly review. |
| **Showstopper** | A high risk with no adequate control. The idea is fixed or parked until it is resolved. |
| **Sponsor** | The senior executive who owns the programme, has budget authority and makes the gate decisions. |
| **Strategic bet** | An idea aimed at a lasting advantage, with more uncertainty than a table stake. |
| **Table stake** | Something competitors already do, which you need to do well to keep up. |
| **Use case** | One specific task or process where Gen AI could help, such as drafting product descriptions. |
| **Value** | How much an idea matters to the business. One of the two scoring axes. |
| **Value chain** | The sequence of activities that turns inputs into products or services and delivers them to customers. |
| **Workforce transition** | The plan for how roles change: who is reskilled, redeployed or hired. |
